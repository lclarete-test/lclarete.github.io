Patient Research · Netnography · Brazil, 2016

Acute and chronic respiratory diseases

The study examined the social media landscape of respiratory diseases, especially allergies, covering colds and flu, rhinitis, sinusitis, asthma and bronchitis. Claritin was included in the treatment and antihistamine analysis.

Study objective

Understand respiratory diseases, especially allergies, in social media

The study built an overview of acute and chronic respiratory conditions, focusing on colds and flu, rhinitis, sinusitis, asthma and bronchitis.

Specific objectives

  • Describe acute and chronic allergies.
  • Identify periods of higher incidence.
  • Characterize the people posting and commenting.
  • Identify information sources used by patients and families.
  • Map difficulties from symptoms through treatment maintenance.
  • Provide an overview of the main medications discussed.
Methodology

Netnography

Digital behavior, interactions, values and everyday experiences were observed in preselected information hubs where people gathered around shared health interests, including blogs, groups, forums and communities.

13,223posts in the respiratory corpus
2,225posts in medication analysis
6,019posts in symptom analysis

Capture

Search terms covered diseases, symptoms, medications and lexical variants across selected digital hubs.

Screen

Commercial noise and automated content were separated from experiences, questions and peer discussion.

Classify

Posts were organized by channel, topic, symptom, trigger, disease, audience, treatment and information source.

Analyze

The analysis compared conversation volume, language, information flows, treatment classes, brand mentions and interaction patterns.

Synthesize

Patterns were consolidated into findings, audience profiles, content needs, recommendations and product hypotheses.

Safety triage

Potential adverse events, atypical reactions, off-label use and excessive consumption were routed through the pharmacovigilance process.

Capture→Screen→Classify→Analyze→Synthesize→Safety triage

Patterns affecting reassessment

  • Loss of perceived effectiveness prompted requests for alternatives.
  • Concern about dependence was prominent in decongestant discussion.
  • Drowsiness and feeling “drugged” were the most discussed side effects.
  • Patients often tested multiple brands or classes before settling into a routine.
  • Claritin ranked fifth among the medications mentioned in the report.

Content interests and unmet interaction needs

These needs were reported separately from the persona frameworks.

Disease and prevention

  • Maintaining a trigger-free environment
  • Winter health care
  • Comparing respiratory conditions
  • Consequences and disease progression

Treatment

  • Less aggressive and non-dependent options
  • New medications and loss of effect over time
  • Interactions with alcohol and other drugs
  • Self-medication risks and when to seek a specialist

Symptom relief

  • Healthy routines for respiratory conditions
  • Home and alternative treatments
  • Foods associated with immune support
  • Soups, teas and practical relief content

Unmet interaction needs

  • Spaces to ask physicians or experienced patients questions
  • Question-and-answer tools connecting respiratory, skin and pulmonary symptoms to allergies
  • Reference mothers who could share routines and facilitate caregiver discussion

Product hypothesis

An interactive symptom tool could support symptom identification, suggest which specialist to seek and allow users to record results and monitor symptom evolution.

Implications

Applications documented in the project materials.

Seasonal timing

Relate weather changes to peaks in respiratory conversation to time educational content and media.

Condition differentiation

Explain symptoms and causes, separating acute and chronic allergies and comparing allergies with viral respiratory illnesses.

Specialist guidance

Connect persistent or concerning symptoms with the appropriate route to general practitioners, ENT specialists, pulmonologists or dermatologists.

Question-led education

Use recurring questions about medication, interactions, dependence and administration to define educational priorities.
